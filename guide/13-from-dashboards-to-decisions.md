# 13 · From dashboards to decisions

*The view*

**More metrics ≠ more understanding. Don't start with dashboards. Start with questions.**

![Left, a dashboard: forty small panels of lines and gauges, no question answered. Right, a decision view: the ten signals in rows, each with a state, a trend, a risk level and an owner, and the questions they answer. Between them, the five questions to ask before any dashboard.](../figures/13-from-dashboards-to-decisions.png)

### A dashboard is not an understanding

Forty panels on a screen tell an engineer where to look and tell a decision-maker nothing. The same platform, seen through ten signals with a state, a trend, a risk and an owner, tells both what to do next.

### Two levels, one source

Technical observability is deep, per service, for the people who investigate. Decision-oriented observability is narrow, per question, for the people who decide. They read the same data. The mistake is to show the first to the second.

### Five questions before any dashboard

- What do we need to know?
- Why do we need to know it?
- Who needs the information?
- What decision will it support?
- What happens if we don't know?
